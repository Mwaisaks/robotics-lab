# Pitch: the problem and the robotics (Joshua)

For the slides and the 1-2 minute problem/robotics part of the talk. Honesty rule: we only claim what the build does.

- **One kit, many students.** In many Kenyan schools one robotics kit is shared by a whole class, so most learners watch while a few build. Robotics Lab gives every student their own hands-on practice on a school laptop, a phone or a VR headset, before they touch the real kit.
- **Level 1, Sort the parts: a robot has a workspace.** Students sort parts into bins while a robot arm follows the part they're holding. The arm turns red when a part is out of reach, so they learn that a robot can only work inside the area it can reach.
- **Level 2, Program the robot: sequence, direction, loops.** Students build a program from FORWARD, BACK, LEFT, RIGHT and REPEAT blocks and RUN it on a 5x5 grid with obstacles. They learn to plan a sequence, to turn from the robot's point of view, to shorten a program with a loop, and to debug when the robot bumps into something.
- **Level 3, Light it up: a circuit is a loop.** Students wire battery, switch, resistor and LED, then press POWER. They learn that current needs a closed loop, that an LED only works one way round, and that a resistor protects it. It's a simplified circuit model, and the screen says so.
- **Build the robot: a robot is made of subsystems.** Students pick up 2 wheels, 2 motors and a controller board and snap each onto its spot on the chassis. A part dropped in the wrong spot goes back, with a hint. When all 5 are on, they press GO and the robot drives a short path (a simplified demo). They learn the main parts of a mobile robot and where each one attaches.
- **Mistakes are the lesson.** Every bump or open circuit is a named mistake, and Robo explains *why* it happened in English or Kiswahili. Students try again straight away at no cost: nothing breaks, and no kit is waiting for them to finish.
- **Why hands-on matters.** Robotics is learned by doing: moving, wiring, testing, fixing. Hands in the headset (or the mouse and touch screen in a browser) keep students doing instead of watching. Their teacher sees who is stuck and on what, so the class's time on the real kit goes further.
